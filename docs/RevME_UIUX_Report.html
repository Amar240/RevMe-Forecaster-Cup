1Executive Scorecard

The platform's visual foundation is stronger than most student projects ever get: a real token system, dark mode, role-aware navigation, skeleton loaders, and thoughtful empty states. The gap is not polish — it's pedagogy. The app reports scores; it does not yet teach. Students see what their MAPE is, never why, and the richest learning moment in the entire competition — the week actuals arrive — currently has no screen of its own.

One-sentence verdict: RevME successfully runs the competition — the redesign opportunity is to make it explain the competition back to the student, because research consistently shows students' top demand from analytics dashboards is "grades and the reasons behind them," and feedback loops are what make forecasting competitions educational rather than just competitive.

2Design System Audit

Reviewed: tailwind.config.ts, globals.css, 18 UI primitives in components/ui.

Strong What's working

  • Full CSS-variable token system — semantic colors (surface/text/status), radius scale, shadow scale, light + dark themes. This is professional-grade structure.
  • Distinct brand palette — navy #1F4E8C + gold #C89B3C reads "academic institution," which is exactly right for the audience. Medal tokens (gold/silver/bronze) and an 8-step chart palette show real foresight.
  • Typography intent — serif display / sans body / mono for numbers is a premium pairing most student apps never attempt.
  • Component hygiene — skeletons, confirm-dialog, alert-banner, access-denied, page-loader all exist as primitives. Loading and error states are designed, not accidental.

Fix Gaps & drift

  • Hardcoded hex escaping the system — sparkline strokes use #16a34a/#dc2626 instead of tokens; skeletons use bg-gray-200 instead of bg-muted. Dark mode will drift.
  • No number typography rule — MAPE tables should use font-mono + tabular-nums everywhere; currently inconsistent, so columns of scores don't align.
  • Missing primitives — no Tooltip (needed badly for MAPE explainers), no Tabs component used on Leaderboards (hand-rolled buttons), no Stat/KPI card primitive despite the pattern appearing 10+ times.
  • The serif display font barely appears inside the app — headers are all sans. The "premium" font is reserved for the landing page; bringing it to dashboard H1s and big numbers would unify the brand.

3Screen-by-Screen Findings

Student Dashboard good bonesmissing the point

  • The current-round hero with countdown, deadline, and submitted/not-submitted state is the best screen in the app. Warning count shown as "1 / 3 before disqualification" is excellent expectation-setting.
  • Your rank and score are nowhere on the dashboard. The single thing every student checks daily requires two extra clicks. The dashboard answers "did I submit?" but not "how am I doing?" or "what should I learn next?"
  • Recent Activity averages Occupancy and ADR across all three markets into one number pair (e.g. "72.4 | $185"). Averaging Nashville with Dubai is statistically meaningless and quietly teaches students bad habits — show per-market chips or last-round error instead.
  • Post-deadline, pre-scoring is dead air: nothing tells the student "actuals arrive Tuesday, scores Wednesday." Waiting states are unused teaching moments.

Submit Forecast great flowblind form

  • Review step → lock warning → confirm is exactly right for an irreversible action. Lock-state messaging (paused/closed/not-open) is thorough and human.
  • Students forecast with zero context on screen. No historical actuals, no last-round result, no market intel — even though the platform already stores all three (Actual, PredictionError, MarketInfo). They're guessing into empty number inputs; the real work happens in a spreadsheet somewhere else. This is the single biggest ease-of-use and learning miss.
  • No draft state. 12 numbers, one shot, permanent lock. A browser crash mid-entry loses everything; anxiety is built into the design.
  • No sanity checks. Typing occupancy 9.5 instead of 95, or an ADR 3× last week's actual, submits silently. A soft "this is 40% below last observed — intentional?" nudge would prevent most heartbreak (and support tickets).
  • Deadlines shown in ET only, for a competition with teams in Dubai and Hamburg.

Leaderboards celebratoryrank without reason

  • Podium, "You" badge, row highlight, sparklines, team/university toggle, per-metric tabs — engaging and well-featured.
  • MAPE is never explained. The core number of the entire competition has no tooltip, no legend, no "lower is better" hint. First-time students must infer the scoring system from context.
  • Teams ranked 8–30 see a wall of rows and learn nothing. Research on learning dashboards warns that raw social comparison demotivates lower-ranked students. Add a "your neighborhood" view: gap to the team above you, percentile, and personal trend — competition framing for the top, progress framing for everyone else.
  • Before any round is published, students can see ranked rows with all values as "--": position without evidence, which reads as broken.
  • Green/red sparkline is the only trend cue — colorblind users get nothing. Add ▲▼ deltas.

My Scores + Score Details rich datanumbers ≠ insight

  • Trend chart, per-round expansion, market filter, CSV export, per-prediction error table — the data layer is generous. Two separate nav items ("My Scores", "Score Details") for overlapping content confuses the mental model; merge into one Scores hub with tabs.
  • Everything is descriptive, nothing is diagnostic. The page shows occupancy APE 8.2% but never: "You over-forecast ADR in all 3 markets for 3 straight rounds" (bias), "Your week+2 error is 2.1× your week+1 error" (horizon difficulty), or "Cohort median this round was 6.1% — you beat it" (reference frame). All computable from data already in PredictionError.

Market Info hidden gem

  • Demand drivers, supply notes, risks, strategy hints, resource links, per-round updates — this is genuinely strong curriculum content with a proper data model behind it.
  • It's stranded. Nothing links it to the submit flow (where it would inform forecasts) or to results (where "what changed this round" would explain misses). The round-update headline (MarketRoundUpdate) is the raw material for a post-round debrief — currently it just sits on this page.

Supervisor Experience thin

  • Dashboard = three counts + a team list. Scores page adds team cards with mini trend charts. Functional, but a professor running this as coursework needs: which teams haven't submitted (with hours left), which are trending worse, what the class-wide common error was, and something to project in class after each round.
  • Faculty are the adoption decision-makers — the supervisor view is where "premium" earns its keep.

4The Learning Gap — Why This Matters

Three findings from learning-analytics and forecasting research map directly onto RevME:

Feedback must be actionable

Dashboards improve outcomes when they tell students what to do differently, not just how they scored. RevME currently stops at scores.

Reference frames > rankings

Students interpret data through comparison points — their own past performance, or cohort medians. Pure rank-based social comparison demotivates the bottom half. RevME offers only ranks.

The feedback loop IS the pedagogy

In forecasting competitions specifically, the learning happens when participants confront prediction vs. outcome and revise their method. RevME has no screen where that confrontation happens.

The insight: a weekly cadence of forecast → actuals → debrief → improve is a textbook deliberate-practice loop. RevME has built the first two steps. The redesign should build the second two.

5Redesign Proposals

P1 — The Round Debrief flagship

A new page, auto-generated the moment a round's leaderboard is published. This is the highest-value addition possible with zero new data collection — every element below already exists in PredictionError, Actual, ScoreAggregate, and MarketRoundUpdate.

  • Per-market forecast-vs-actual with plain-language deltas; biggest miss and best call surfaced first.
  • Cohort median + percentile (reference frame that motivates the whole distribution, not just the podium).
  • "What moved the market" pulls the existing MarketRoundUpdate for that round — connecting admin curriculum content to student results automatically.
  • "Pattern watch" = simple rule-based bias detection (consistent over/under by market/metric across rounds). No ML needed — three SQL queries.
  • Notify via the existing notification system: "Your Round 4 debrief is ready."

P2 — Forecast Workspace (Submit redesign)

Turn the blind form into a split-screen decision environment: inputs on the left, evidence on the right. Draft autosave and soft sanity checks remove the anxiety.

  • Evidence panel per market: trailing actuals sparkline, your last error there, and the MarketInfo quick insights — all existing data.
  • Draft autosave (localStorage first, server draft later) fixes the one-shot anxiety; the existing review/lock step stays as-is.
  • Soft validation nudges (never blocking): large deviation from trailing actuals, occupancy>100 typo patterns, ADR order-of-magnitude checks. This directly reduces the orphan-submission and support-ticket load found in the code review.
  • Show deadline in ET and the student's local timezone.

P3 — Scores Hub with an Insight Layer

  • Merge "My Scores" + "Score Details" into one hub: Overview / By round / By market / Export tabs.
  • Add three diagnostic widgets: Bias meter (over/under-forecast tendency per metric), Horizon split (week+1 vs week+2 accuracy — teaches uncertainty growth), Cohort band on the trend chart (your line vs. anonymized median/quartiles).
  • Every widget gets a one-line plain-language takeaway. Rule: no number without a sentence.

P4 — Leaderboard Reframing

  • Keep the podium (it's good). Below it, add a "Your position" band: gap to the next rank ("0.4% behind #6"), percentile, movement arrow with ▲▼ text (not color alone).
  • MAPE tooltip + a permanent "lower is better" microcopy. One-line formula on hover: average % miss across all your predictions.
  • Hide fully-empty ranked rows pre-publication; show a "Scores land Wednesday" state instead of dashes.

P5 — Supervisor Coaching View

  • Team health matrix: rows = teams; columns = submitted-this-round (with hours-left countdown), round MAPE, trend, bias flag, warnings. Red/amber/green at a glance — with icons, not color alone.
  • Class insights panel: cohort-wide common error this round ("14 of 18 teams over-forecast Hamburg"), best forecast of the week (celebrate method, not just rank).
  • Projectable debrief: one-click, clean full-screen summary of the round for classroom discussion — this is the feature professors will talk about to other professors.

P6 — Learning Micro-Content

  • Glossary primitives: hoverable definitions for MAPE, ADR, Occupancy, RevPAR, comp set — used everywhere those terms appear.
  • Use waiting states: after submission and between rounds, surface one short "how professional revenue managers think" tip or a relevant market-info card instead of blank confirmation.
  • Rules page → "Guidelines + Help" already exists; add a 5-minute "How to make your first forecast" onboarding path for round 1 (biggest anxiety moment of the season).

6Premium Polish Checklist

  • Motion: framer-motion is already a dependency — bring subtle entrance/stagger animations from the landing page into dashboard cards, and animate rank changes on the leaderboard.
  • Celebration states: after submitting (a real "locked in — good luck" moment), on personal-best MAPE, on rank-up. Cheap to build, huge for felt quality.
  • Serif display font on dashboard H1s and hero numbers — unify with the landing page brand.
  • Tabular numerals (font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums) on every score, table, and countdown.
  • Replace all hardcoded grays/greens/reds with tokens; audit dark mode on Scores + Leaderboards.
  • Mobile tables → card lists on Leaderboards and Scores below md; students will check ranks from phones.
  • Trend cues never color-only: pair ▲▼/text with every green/red.
  • Timezone dual-display everywhere a deadline appears.
